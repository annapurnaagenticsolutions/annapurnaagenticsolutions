# Chapter VII — Salt Library: Research-to-Design Brief

## Narrative premise

**The Salt Library** is an original, cliffside collection point where route ledgers, oral accounts, weather observations, and habitat notes arrive on different schedules. It is not a temple, scripture archive, historical institution, or a reconstruction of a real coast. The player’s task is to make a shared warning legible without pretending that one kind of evidence cancels the others.

## Play loop

The first visit follows a **trace → compare → annotate → share** rhythm. Star-thread Sight reveals the relationship among the three caskets; the Archive Shard illuminates missing connections; and a new **Field Annotation** technique lets Ila mark an uncertainty instead of forcing a false conclusion. The final “public memory” decision offers trade-offs among trade access, local testimony, and habitat warning. It raises a trait but preserves a clear counterweight in the Chronicle.

> “Knowledge source” debates in classical Indian philosophy are diverse and contested; the Salt Library turns that plurality into a fictional practice of comparison rather than a game answer key.[1]

## Character writing rules

The **Library Steward** asks precise questions: “What does this record show? What does it leave uncounted? Who needs to read it before the tide changes?” Tara’s sixth milestone focuses on return routes and learning from people already keeping a place legible. Aruna speaks about evidence that names its uncertainty, while Laya speaks about shared attention as a practiced social rhythm. Somavrat’s decision treats records as public infrastructure rather than possession.

## Terrain and atmosphere

The Salt Library follows the Rasa Engine diagonal: lower-left salt grass and tide flats, middle-stage weathered copper wayfinding plates, upper-right pale cliff ledger stacks. The mineral palette combines salt-parchment, oxidized copper, tide blue, dune jade, and a small saffron mark only when a missing relation becomes visible. **Hemanta** introduces dry salt wind and long shadows; **Varsha** introduces muffled pages, wet steps, and lower route visibility; no seasonal state grants a health penalty or simulates ritual practice.

## Verification criteria

| Requirement | Visible implementation test |
|---|---|
| Respectful framing | HUD and Chronicle call the records original field notes; no sacred text, ritual, deity, or real community is portrayed as an interaction object. |
| Evidence pluralism | Three caskets have different use and limitation copy; the final decision retains all three. |
| Terrain fidelity | The first frame visibly includes salt grass, cliff steps, copper instrument, tide blue route line, and a diagonal ascent. |
| Seasonality | The existing six-season control changes wind/visibility language and the replay record order. |
| Replayability | A second route offers shifted casket order and an uncertainty count record. |
