# Chapter IX — The Confluence Table

## Purpose

**The Confluence Table** is an original fictional public-route workshop where Ila brings the Countermark Lens into contact with several incoming paths. It is not a reconstruction of a historical council, court, school, or sacred setting. Its playable question is practical and bounded: **when river, ridge, and shore evidence meet, what route can be shared while keeping its source, condition, and return requirement visible?**

The chapter applies the project’s evidence-pluralism research without claiming to reproduce any school of thought. The source distinction remains useful because accounts of classical Indian epistemology foreground the provenance of cognition and discuss perception, inference, and testimony as important candidate knowledge sources.[1] Scholarship on Indian epistemic cultures also frames public knowledge as involving plural standpoints and revisable dialogue, supporting a game mechanic of amendment rather than victory.[2]

| Element | Chapter IX expression | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Source | River Gauge, Ridge Signal, Shore Record | These are fictional route instruments, not historical objects. |
| Condition | Water level, wind corridor, salinity window | The system represents a dramatic route constraint, not real forecasting. |
| Public consequence | Who can use, question, or revise a route record | The final choice never maps onto a real community or policy. |
| Revision | A return condition visibly accompanies the published braid | Revision is a fictional gameplay action, not a legal or ritual procedure. |

## Spatial target

The registered **Confluence Table** concept plate is the scene reference: an indigo-dusk open-air terrace with three diagonal incoming arms, a copper-and-parchment radial table, restrained jade refuge surfaces, terracotta route steps, and Yantra Saffron only at active insight points. The route must read from **lower-left arrival shelter → central public table → upper-right return horizon**. The three source stations form a broad triangle around the central instrument, leaving clean walking corridors and visible landmarks for the cinematic camera.

## Play loop

1. **Gather:** Use focus pulses to collect the River Gauge, Ridge Signal, and Shore Record.
2. **Situate:** Walk to the public table and place each source beside the condition it can actually speak to.
3. **Revise:** Receive an original return note from the Confluence Steward; use the Countermark Lens to expose a route’s change condition.
4. **Share:** Choose which public route braid is released first. Every option keeps an explicit return condition rather than presenting a perfect solution.

## Earned systems

Completion awards **Route Braid**, a route-planning technique that carries a visible source-condition-return triad into the Chronicle. Tara’s six-knot journal concludes with an **unbound line** rather than an additional knot: she shifts from giving a route instruction to asking Ila to identify the next reader who must be able to amend it. A three-order seasonal replay changes which source must be placed first, while maintaining the same three source categories.

## Verification criteria

The locked chapter states its Countermark Lens prerequisite. An active chapter visibly shows three source stations, a central table, source/condition/public-consequence progress, the Confluence Steward, and Tara’s unbound-line prompt. Completion writes Route Braid, an amendable route decision, a seasonal replay record, and a Chapter X signal to the HUD, field map, Chronicle, campaign recap, and landing continuation state. Reduced-motion and high-contrast states must preserve all reading-critical elements.
