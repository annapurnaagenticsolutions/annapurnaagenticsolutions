# Chapter XI — The Living Survey

## Original premise

The Living Survey is the campaign’s next **fictional** field question. After Ila has published and amended a route, later readers return to the basin with their own marks. The chapter asks how an earlier line, its amendment, and a newly observed variance can remain comparable without being forced into a single final map.

## Research boundary

The design uses only the broad research constraint that ecological and cartographic records are mediated, situated, and varied across time and place. It does **not** reproduce a historical survey, territorial practice, community, script, instrument, or sacred narrative. All objects—survey threads, variance marks, terrain register, and the Surveyor of Returns—are original fiction.

## Intended play loop

| Step | Player action | Persistent outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Return | Carry the Revision Compass into a basin that has changed since the last route edition. | The former line remains visible. |
| Mark | Read three new field conditions without erasing their distinct frames. | Three variance marks enter the Chronicle. |
| Compare | Place earlier line, amendment, and field mark around the copper survey instrument. | The player sees where records agree and differ. |
| Publish variance | Choose an accountable notation for the next traveler. | The route becomes revisable without being declared complete. |

## Visual target

Until a dedicated concept plate is available, the landing page presents a **procedural Rasa Engine instrument preview**: a dark indigo survey basin, parchment route line, jade amendment arc, restrained saffron field mark, copper armillary rings, and a lower-left water edge rising diagonally to the upper-right instrument. This preview is intentionally distinct from existing chapter art and communicates future direction without presenting an image as a historical reconstruction.

## Verification contract

The eventual playable chapter must preserve the three-layer distinction between earlier route, amendment, and new variance. Its completion may not claim a final authoritative map; it must keep a reader-facing return point visible in the route record.
