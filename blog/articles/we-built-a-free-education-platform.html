We Built a Free Education Platform for India. Here's What We Learned.

Annapurna Agentic Solutions · Archive

Theme: The Education

Length: ~900 words

Hook: Story — build empathy through a specific narrative

Six months ago, we started building Jigyasu — a free education platform for India. Not "freemium." Not "free for the first 30 days." Free. Forever. For everyone.

Today it's live, with 22 Indian languages, 100+ interactive lessons, and 8 learning worlds. No ads. No data collection. No premium tier. No "upgrade to unlock" buttons.

Here's what we learned.

Lesson 1: "Free" is a design constraint, not a pricing strategy.

When you decide something is free forever, every decision changes. You can't rely on subscription revenue, so you design for minimal infrastructure costs. You can't lock features behind paywalls, so you design for universal access. You can't assume users have fast internet, so you design for 2G and offline.

The constraint produced better design. Offline-first architecture, lightweight pages, shared-device support — these weren't features we added. They were consequences of being free.

Lesson 2: India is not a market to "expand into." It's the starting point.

Most ed-tech platforms are built in Silicon Valley and "localized" for India. They translate the UI, swap some examples, and call it "India-ready."

We started with India. The first language wasn't English — it was Hindi. The examples aren't American — they're Indian. Rupees, not dollars. Rotis, not sandwiches. Monsoons, not snow. Indian scientists, not Western ones.

When a child in a village opens Jigyasu, she doesn't see a foreign platform translated for her. She sees a platform built for her.

Lesson 3: 22 languages is a technical decision, not a marketing claim.

India has 22 official scheduled languages. Supporting all of them from day one isn't a feature — it's an architecture. We use i18n (internationalization) as a core system, not an add-on. Every string, every lesson, every interaction is language-agnostic.

The result: adding a new language takes days, not months. And when a community wants to contribute a translation, the system is ready.

Lesson 4: The website is the app.

We made a deliberate choice: website-first, not app-first. In India, where shared phones are common and app stores have barriers (download size, permissions, account requirements), a website that works on any browser is more accessible than a native app.

A link shared through WhatsApp can reach a child in a village. No install. No account. No friction.

Lesson 5: Sustainability without subscriptions is possible — but requires creativity.

How do you sustain something that's free forever? Not through ads (we refuse to show ads to children). Not through data (we collect zero personal data). Not through a premium tier (there isn't one).

The answer: CSR partnerships, individual contributions, translation sponsorship, infrastructure sponsorship, and school/NGO partnerships. Indian companies are required by law to spend 2% of profit on CSR. Education is a qualifying category. The model works — but it requires relationship-building, not growth-hacking.

Lesson 6: "No poor version and rich version" is a principle, not a feature.

Most ed-tech platforms have a free tier (limited) and a paid tier (full). The free tier is intentionally degraded — fewer lessons, lower quality, ads. The message is: if you can't pay, you get less.

We rejected this. Everything is free. Forever. For everyone. There is no poor version. There is no rich version. There is one version, and it's beautiful.

This is not a feature. It is a principle. Equity means the village child and the city child get the same experience.

Lesson 7: Wonder is the curriculum.

Our values are not "engagement," "retention," or "conversion." They are: Wonder, Equity, Respect, Patience, Joy, Identity.

We don't have grades. We don't have timers. We don't have leaderboards. We have interactive simulations where you can see gravity bend space, watch photosynthesis happen, feel fractions as fair sharing.

If learning is not joyful, something is wrong with the design, not the learner.

What's next.

Jigyasu is live. It's not perfect. It's not finished. It will never be finished — that's the point. Education is an ongoing project, not a product to ship.

We're now building STEM Labs (5 interactive science labs), AI Concepts Lab (37 AI concepts with visual explanations), and storybooks that teach values through Indian stories.

All free. All forever. All for India.

The question we keep asking: if education is a right, not a privilege, what does the platform look like?

It looks like this.
