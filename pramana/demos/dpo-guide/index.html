PRIVACY LEADERSHIP GUIDE

Build a clear path from questions to accountable decisions.

This guide supports general planning for teams responsible for privacy governance. It does not describe internal Pramana methods or replace qualified legal advice.

Four leadership questions

What processing do we oversee?

Can business owners describe the purposes, systems and parties involved?

What is operative now?

Do teams verify the correct provision, commencement and relevant official changes?

Who owns the next step?

Are decisions and unresolved questions routed to accountable people?

What should be retained?

Are records appropriate to purpose, access, retention and applicable obligations—and can the team distinguish what has been implemented, what can be evidenced and what remains unresolved?

Common questions to route for review

These examples preserve familiar business and technology questions as prompts. They do not determine legal applicability, compliance or the answer for a specific organisation.

A small clinic or startup

Size alone does not determine a Data Fiduciary role or Significant Data Fiduciary status. Identify who determines purpose and means for each activity, and check any official notification.

Sharing a lab report through a messaging app

Review the purpose, recipients, roles, safeguards, service-provider terms and any relevant health-sector requirements. The channel name alone does not answer the legal question.

Student or learner data

If children’s personal data may be involved, review Section 9 with Rules 10 and 12, their commencement and exemption conditions. Do not assume every education analytics use is prohibited or exempt.

An erasure request

The Act addresses correction and erasure in Section 12. Check commencement, the request’s facts and any applicable retention requirement; there is no general DPDP 72-hour erasure deadline.

Aadhaar identifiers in records

Check what identifier is collected, how it is used and which current Aadhaar or UIDAI instrument applies. A field label alone does not establish a violation.

A cloud region outside India

Review Section 16, Rules 15 and 13(4) where relevant, applicable sector instruments and commencement. Do not treat the DPDP Act as a fixed country blacklist.

An AI or agent cites a legal rule

Verify the official source, provision, version, commencement and conditions. Generated text is not a legal determination. For data used in decisions, review Section 8(3) where it applies; the Act does not create a universal human-review right.

Use the guide as a starting point

Adapt the discussion to your organisation’s real activities and responsibility model. Do not use a general guide to infer a legal exemption, role or compliance status.
